About Samuel

Experience that goes beyond the qualification.

My working life has always been about people. Long before coaching, I worked in senior retail sales — learning to read what someone really needs beneath what they first say, to earn trust quickly, and to guide a decision without ever pushing. Those instincts never left me.

I joined the NHS in the early 2000s and spent over two decades there, holding various roles across my Trust — but the work was always about people. Much of it was alongside carers in mental health services, making sure their voices and concerns were heard while they supported their loved ones through difficult times. I also worked in equality and diversity, and served as a Cultural Inclusion Ambassador. In time I found systemic family therapy, working within the service on secondment and later full time. That training shaped how I understand people — not in isolation, but within the relationships, families, and systems that surround them. I have drawn on those systemic principles and techniques in my practice ever since. Today I also run a commercial business, where looking after people well is the work itself.

Alongside this, I have served on management committees for third sector organisations — work that gave me a real understanding of governance, accountability, and the particular pressures faced by mission-driven organisations running on limited resources. I know the world charity and community leaders live in, because I have sat at that table.

So the thread running through everything I have done is the same one that sits at the heart of coaching — understanding people, earning their trust, and helping them move toward what they need. My ILM Level 5 formalised a craft I have been refining my whole adult life.

Coaching, for me, is not a technique. It is a practice of drawing out what is already within a person — the counsel, the clarity, the capacity — and helping them trust it.

Who qualifies for the concession rate?

Students — Full-time students at a UK higher education institution. Bring a valid student ID to your first session, or provide a named referral from a university pastoral team, student union, or academic tutor when booking.


NHS Staff — Current NHS employees or those who have left the NHS within the past 24 months. Verified by NHS email address used at booking, or NHS staff ID or recent payslip shown at the first session.


Faith Community Referrals — Individuals referred by a named leader within a recognised Abrahamic faith congregation — including Hebrew Roots and Israelite communities, Christian churches, and Muslim mosques. The referral must come directly from a pastor, elder, imam, rabbi, or equivalent community leader. Self-referral under this category is not accepted.
